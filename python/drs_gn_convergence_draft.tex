\section{Purpose and status}

This note separates three statements that must not be conflated:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item an exact, fixed-metric nonconvex Douglas--Rachford splitting (DRS)
        result inherited through a linear change of coordinates;
  \item a conditional perturbation result for inexact local solves; and
  \item practical heuristics---finite Gauss--Newton (GN) or
        Levenberg--Marquardt (LM) solves, refreshed curvature metrics, and
        Nesterov-like proposals---whose compliance with the conditional result
        remains to be proved or measured.
\end{enumerate}

Only the first result is currently unconditional under its stated assumptions.
The second result identifies a sufficient proof target.  It does not by itself
show that a fixed number of GN steps satisfies that target.

\section{Landmark-owned consensus formulation}

Let cluster $i\in\{1,\ldots,K\}$ own a disjoint set of complete landmark
tracks.  Its local variables are camera copies $u_i$ and owned landmarks
$\ell_i$, and its reprojection objective is $f_i(u_i,\ell_i)$.  Define the
reduced camera objective
\begin{equation}
  \bar f_i(u_i)=\inf_{\ell_i} f_i(u_i,\ell_i),
  \label{eq:reduced-objective}
\end{equation}
and stack all local camera copies into $u=(u_1,\ldots,u_K)$.  The consensus
subspace is
\begin{equation}
  \Cset=\{u:\ u_{i,c}=u_{j,c}\text{ whenever camera }c
  \text{ occurs in clusters }i\text{ and }j\}.
\end{equation}
The decomposed problem is
\begin{equation}
  \min_u\ \Phi(u):=F(u)+G(u),
  \qquad
  F(u)=\sum_{i=1}^K\bar f_i(u_i),
  \qquad
  G(u)=\iota_{\Cset}(u).
  \label{eq:split-problem}
\end{equation}

Equation~\eqref{eq:reduced-objective} is mathematically convenient but places
real requirements on landmark elimination.  Smoothness of $\bar f_i$ is local,
not automatic: it follows near a selected landmark minimizer when the landmark
first-order condition is regular and the relevant landmark Hessian is
nonsingular.  Gauge freedoms, degeneracy, robust losses, and changes between
local minimizer branches can violate these conditions.  A theorem may instead
retain $(u_i,\ell_i)$ explicitly, but then the consensus split and its prox must
be restated in that larger space.

\section{Fixed block metric as a coordinate transform}

Let $M\succ0$ be a \emph{constant} block-diagonal camera metric.  For the
implementation, each active camera block is $9\times9$.  Introduce
\begin{equation}
  z=M^{1/2}u,
  \qquad
  \widetilde F(z)=F(M^{-1/2}z),
  \qquad
  \widetilde G(z)=G(M^{-1/2}z).
  \label{eq:metric-transform}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{prox}^{M}_{\gamma F}(s)
  =M^{-1/2}\operatorname{prox}_{\gamma\widetilde F}(M^{1/2}s),
\end{equation}
and similarly for $G$.  Thus fixed-metric DRS in $u$ is Euclidean DRS in
$z$.  The metric projection of a reflected state $r=(r_i)_i$ onto consensus
is separable by camera:
\begin{equation}
  v_c=
  \left(\sum_{i:c\in i}M_{i,c}\right)^{-1}
  \sum_{i:c\in i}M_{i,c}r_{i,c}.
  \label{eq:metric-consensus}
\end{equation}
With $r_i=2u_i-s_i$, this is the block-metric consensus used by the solver.

\section{Exact fixed-metric DRS}

For clarity, use the convention
\begin{align}
  u_k &\in \operatorname{prox}^{M}_{\gamma F}(s_k),
  \\
  v_k &\in \operatorname{prox}^{M}_{\gamma G}(2u_k-s_k),
  \\
  s_{k+1} &= s_k+\lambda(v_k-u_k),
  \qquad \lambda\in(0,2),
  \label{eq:exact-drs}
\end{align}
and define the fixed-point residual $r_k=u_k-v_k$.

\begin{assumption}[Exact fixed-metric setting]
\label{ass:exact}
The following hold in the transformed coordinates of
Equation~\eqref{eq:metric-transform}.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $\widetilde F$ has an $L$-Lipschitz continuous gradient on a set
        containing the generated level set.
  \item $\widetilde G$ is proper and lower semicontinuous.
  \item Problem~\eqref{eq:split-problem} admits a solution.
  \item The proximal points in Equation~\eqref{eq:exact-drs} are computed
        exactly, and the same metric $M$ and stepsize $\gamma$ are used at
        every iteration.
  \item The stepsize satisfies $\gamma<(2-\lambda)/(2L)$ in the nonconvex
        case.  For $\lambda=1$, this reduces to $\gamma<1/(2L)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{assumption}

The last bound is the simplified nonconvex range in the DRE analysis of
Themelis, Stella, and Patrinos; a sharper range can be stated if a
hypoconvexity modulus is available.

\begin{theorem}[Exact fixed-metric subsequential convergence]
\label{thm:exact}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:exact}, the metric DRS iterates satisfy
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\normM{r_k}^2<\infty,
  \qquad
  \normM{r_k}\to0.
  \label{eq:residual-summable}
\end{equation}
Every accumulation point shared by $(u_k)$ and $(v_k)$ is stationary for
$F+G$.  If the objective is level bounded, the transformed DRS sequence is
bounded.  Moreover,
\begin{equation}
  \min_{0\leq j<k}\normM{r_j}
  =O(k^{-1/2}).
  \label{eq:best-residual-rate}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply the linear transformation in Equation~\eqref{eq:metric-transform}.
Assumption~\ref{ass:exact} becomes the smooth-first-term setting for Euclidean
nonconvex DRS.  Sufficient decrease of the Douglas--Rachford envelope gives
for some $c>0$
\begin{equation}
  \Edr(z_{k+1})
  \leq \Edr(z_k)-c\lVert \widetilde r_k\rVert^2,
\end{equation}
where $\widetilde r_k=M^{1/2}r_k$.  The envelope is bounded below by the
problem infimum.  Telescoping proves
Equation~\eqref{eq:residual-summable}; the stationary-cluster-point statement
then follows from closedness of the limiting subdifferential under the exact
proximal inclusions.  Finally,
\begin{equation}
  \min_{0\leq j<k}\lVert\widetilde r_j\rVert^2
  \leq \frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}
  \lVert\widetilde r_j\rVert^2=O(k^{-1}),
\end{equation}
which yields Equation~\eqref{eq:best-residual-rate}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What the rate does not say]
Theorem~\ref{thm:exact} gives a best-iterate fixed-point residual rate.  It
does not establish an $O(1/k)$ objective rate, an accelerated complexity
bound, or superiority of Nesterov proposals over plain DRS.
\end{remark}

\section{A conditional inexact result}

Let $\widehat u_k$ be the result of a finite local nonlinear solve and let
$\widehat v_k$ be its exact metric consensus projection.  Define
\begin{equation}
  \widehat r_k=\widehat u_k-\widehat v_k.
\end{equation}
For differentiable local objectives, a computable proximal optimality defect
is
\begin{equation}
  e_k=\nabla F(\widehat u_k)
      +\frac{1}{\gamma}M(\widehat u_k-s_k).
  \label{eq:prox-defect}
\end{equation}
When landmarks are retained explicitly, $e_k$ must include both camera and
landmark stationarity defects, with no proximal term on unregularized
landmark coordinates.

\begin{assumption}[Perturbed envelope decrease]
\label{ass:inexact}
There exist $c>0$ and a nonnegative summable sequence $(\epsilon_k)$ such that
the inexact iterates admit an envelope-like potential $\widehat{\Edr}_k$
bounded below and
\begin{equation}
  \widehat{\Edr}_{k+1}
  \leq \widehat{\Edr}_k
  -c\normM{\widehat r_k}^2+\epsilon_k,
  \qquad
  \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\epsilon_k<\infty.
  \label{eq:perturbed-descent}
\end{equation}
In addition, $\normMi{e_k}\to0$, and the generated sequence has an
accumulation point.
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional convergence with inexact local solves]
\label{thm:inexact}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:inexact},
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\normM{\widehat r_k}^2<\infty,
  \qquad
  \normM{\widehat r_k}\to0.
\end{equation}
If the consensus projection is exact and the limiting subdifferential is
closed along the convergent subsequence, every accumulation point is
consensus feasible and stationary for $F+G$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Sum Equation~\eqref{eq:perturbed-descent} from $k=0$ to $N$.  Lower
boundedness of $\widehat{\Edr}_{N+1}$ and summability of $\epsilon_k$ bound
$\sum_{k=0}^N\normM{\widehat r_k}^2$ uniformly in $N$.  Hence the residual
vanishes.  Along any convergent subsequence, exact projection and
$\widehat r_k\to0$ give consensus feasibility.  Passing to the limit in the
inexact proximal inclusion, using $e_k\to0$ in the dual metric and closedness
of the limiting subdifferential, gives stationarity.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[This theorem is a target, not yet an implementation theorem]
The main unresolved step is deriving Equation~\eqref{eq:perturbed-descent}
and $e_k\to0$ from the actual finite GN/LM policy.  A fixed number of local
iterations with a fixed termination tolerance generally gives bounded error,
not summable or vanishing error.  Theorem~\ref{thm:inexact} must therefore not
be cited as proving the current implementation until that bridge is supplied.
\end{remark}

\section{Candidate checkable forcing conditions}

The following alternatives should be investigated.  Any one is useful only
after proving that it implies Assumption~\ref{ass:inexact} under the stated
smoothness and regularity conditions.

\subsection{Summable local defects}

Require
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\normMi{e_k}<\infty.
  \label{eq:summable-defect}
\end{equation}
This is clean but usually requires increasingly accurate local solves.

\subsection{Relative error forcing}

Require, for a sufficiently small $\eta$,
\begin{equation}
  \normMi{e_k}\leq\eta\normM{\widehat r_k}.
  \label{eq:relative-error}
\end{equation}
This is more compatible with adaptive local stopping because early outer
iterations permit coarse local solves.  The admissible upper bound on $\eta$
must be derived from the exact DRE decrease constant; it cannot be chosen only
empirically.

\subsection{Direct sufficient-decrease test}

Accept a finite local solve only if a computable merit satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \widehat{\Edr}_{k+1}
  \leq\widehat{\Edr}_k-c\normM{\widehat r_k}^2.
  \label{eq:direct-decrease}
\end{equation}
This moves work from an a priori GN iteration count to an outer acceptance
condition.  It still requires the computed merit to be the same potential
used in the proof.

\subsection{Summable forcing sequence}

Choose local tolerances $\delta_k$ with $\sum_k\delta_k<\infty$ and stop when
\begin{equation}
  \normMi{e_k}\leq\delta_k.
\end{equation}
A practical schedule such as $\delta_k=\delta_0/(k+1)^{1+q}$, $q>0$, is easy
to state but may oversolve late iterations.  Relative forcing is likely more
efficient if it can be proved.

\section{Finite GN/LM proof obligations}

For cluster $i$, the implemented local problem is
\begin{equation}
  q_{i,k}(u_i,\ell_i)=f_i(u_i,\ell_i)
  +\frac{1}{2\gamma}\normM{u_i-s_{i,k}}^2.
\end{equation}
A damped GN/LM step solves a quadratic model, not the proximal problem itself.
To connect finite steps to Equation~\eqref{eq:prox-defect}, the paper must:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item specify the robust loss and whether the model is true GN,
        iteratively reweighted least squares, or another approximation;
  \item bound the difference between the model gradient/Hessian and those of
        $q_{i,k}$ on the relevant level set;
  \item use the LM acceptance ratio or a line search to guarantee decrease in
        the nonlinear local objective;
  \item show how local step norms or gradients control $\lVert e_{i,k}\rVert$;
  \item choose an inner stopping rule that makes the resulting outer error
        relative, summable, or directly decreasing; and
  \item account for switching between landmark minimizer branches and for
        singular local geometry.
\end{enumerate}

The earlier ADMM--GN derivation suggests that sufficiently large proximal
regularization can dominate finite-step model errors.  Its one-step bound is a
useful starting point, but the multi-step cross terms and the exact connection
to the implemented block metric require a fresh proof.  It should not be
imported as a finished theorem.

\section{Changing metrics}

The coordinate argument in Section 3 applies to one fixed $M$.  If $M_k$
changes every outer iteration, then the transformed objective and DRE also
change.  Uniform spectral bounds
\begin{equation}
  mI\preceq M_k\preceq \overline m I
\end{equation}
are useful but do not alone give a common Lyapunov function.  Plausible routes
are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item freeze $M$ after initialization for the proved algorithm;
  \item allow only finitely many metric updates;
  \item require summable variation
        $\sum_k\lVert M_{k+1}-M_k\rVert<\infty$ and absorb the induced
        potential change into $\epsilon_k$; or
  \item construct a metric-independent merit with uniform descent.
\end{enumerate}
Until one route is completed, refreshed metrics are an empirical
variable-metric heuristic, while fixed Jacobi or fixed block metrics are the
theoretically covered variants.

\section{Safeguarded acceleration}

Let $s_k^{\mathrm{trial}}$ be any accelerated proposal, including the
implemented Nesterov-like recurrence.  A convergence-safe abstraction is:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item evaluate the proposal through the same exact or controlled-inexact DRS
        oracle used by the theorem;
  \item accept it only if the proved potential satisfies sufficient decrease;
  \item otherwise take the nominal plain DRS step; and
  \item restart proposal history after rejection if desired.
\end{enumerate}
Under this abstraction, convergence comes from accepted potential decrease
and the plain fallback, not from a general nonconvex Nesterov rate.

The implementation additionally checks the primal objective, restores the
best primal state, changes regularization, and may refresh metrics.  These are
reasonable safeguards, but they are outside Theorem~\ref{thm:exact} unless
their effect is incorporated into a common potential or occurs only finitely
often.

\section{Required diagnostics}

The implementation should record per cluster and outer trial:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $\lVert e_{i,k}\rVert_{M_i^{-1}}$ from
        Equation~\eqref{eq:prox-defect};
  \item camera and landmark gradient norms;
  \item local nonlinear objective before and after the solve;
  \item GN/LM step norm, damping, acceptance ratio, and iteration count;
  \item $\normM{\widehat r_k}$ and the exact metric-consensus residual;
  \item the candidate proof potential before and after the outer step;
  \item metric eigenvalue bounds and $\lVert M_{k+1}-M_k\rVert$; and
  \item whether acceleration, fallback, restoration, or regularization change
        occurred.
\end{enumerate}

Plots should test Equations~\eqref{eq:summable-defect},
\eqref{eq:relative-error}, and \eqref{eq:perturbed-descent} directly.  These
measurements determine which theorem is realistic; they should not be selected
after the proof for cosmetic agreement.

\section{Next proof step}

The next useful theoretical action is not another abstract convergence
statement.  It is to instrument the local proximal defect and determine whether
finite LM solves empirically satisfy relative forcing
Equation~\eqref{eq:relative-error}.  If they do, derive the perturbed DRE bound
and its admissible $\eta$.  If they do not, implement an adaptive inner stopping
rule or retain finite local solves as a clearly identified heuristic.
